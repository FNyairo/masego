maritimeContext: >-
  European ports sit at the front edge of mandatory sustainability regulation. The Corporate
  Sustainability Reporting Directive and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards impose
  detailed disclosure duties, while FuelEU Maritime and the extension of emissions trading to
  shipping place a direct price on carbon. Ports here are therefore both regulated entities and
  the implementation point for rules that apply to the vessels calling at them.
esgChallenges:
  - Complying with rapidly evolving and layered EU regulation
  - Managing carbon costs arising from the Emissions Trading System
  - Coordinating ESG action across fragmented multi-stakeholder port ecosystems
esgOpportunities:
  - First-mover advantage in accessing ESG-linked finance
  - Green corridor partnerships with trading partners inside and outside the EU
  - Influence over standard setting through early, detailed implementation

European ports show what happens when ESG expectations move from voluntary to mandatory. They
supply the study with evidence on reporting burden, data availability, and the practical gap
between regulatory ambition and operational capacity.
